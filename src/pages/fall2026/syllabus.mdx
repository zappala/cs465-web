# Syllabus

## Welcome

CS 465 is designed to give a broad introduction to computer security using CS
224 and CS 260 as prerequisites.

## Course Description

This course is designed to provide a broad introduction to digital security and
privacy. It helps students think about security as a software engineer and
become ready to take further coursework in security and privacy.

Major topics include:

- operating system security
- software security
- network security
- web security
- threat modeling
- usable security and privacy

This course is designed to meet the [BYU Aims](https://aims.byu.edu/): this
course should be spiritually strengthening, intellectually enlargening,
character building, and lead to lifelong learning and service.

## Learning Outcomes

The learning outcomes for this class are:

- **Fluency**: Develop fluency in the language and concepts related to securing
  computing systems

- **Attacks and Defenses**: Be able to execute common attacks against computer
  systems and deploy defenses against those attacks

- **Secure software**: Write software that uses cryptographic primitives to
  securely communicate and store information

- **Security Mindset**: Think like an attacker to identify key assets, risks,
  and mitigations associated with the security and privacy of a system

- **Ethical Practices**: Develop personal values that apply gospel principles
  and ethical practices to to computer security topics that may arise in a
  future career

## Course Requirements

- In Class Quizzes: During most clases, students will complete a quiz based on the reading. Grading is based only on participation. These quizzes are worth 10% of your grade.

- Homework: Homework is used to practice material from the class and get you
  ready for projects. Homework is graded on a participation basis and is worth 10% of your grade.

- Projects: Projects give students hands-on experience with the topics covered in class. Projects are worth 70% of your grade

- Discipleship: A set of assignments that will help you develop into a
  disciple-scholar. These assignments are graded based on demonstrating
  engagement with the topic and are worth 10% of your grade.

## Learning Environment

Class time will be divided into three sections:

- a quiz on the reading, including discussion
- a lecture reviewing key topics from the reading
- questions and discussions on the topic
- doing exercises designed to provide practical insight into key topics

At the end of most units, students will complete a project to demonstrate their mastery of the material covered in the lectures.

There are no exams this semester, because the assignments are designed to cover all of the material. Instead, a final project will have you learn something new beyond what was covered in class.

## Ethical Guidelines

Security techniques can protect people and systems, but they can also cause serious harm. In CS 465, use your skills with honesty, stewardship, respect for others, and concern for human well-being.

- _Work only with authorization._ Test or access only course-provided programs, systems, accounts, and data, or targets explicitly approved by the instructor. Public accessibility, weak security, or an exposed file does not constitute permission. Do not test BYU systems, classmates’ devices or accounts, workplace systems, or public websites.

- _Stay within scope and minimize harm_. Use the least disruptive method necessary. Do not delete or corrupt data, conduct denial-of-service attacks, establish persistence, evade monitoring, or access information beyond what an assignment requires. Run potentially dangerous code only in the specified isolated environment.

- _Handle vulnerability discoveries responsibly_. You should not be identifying vulnerabilities in any systems used in this course, outside the scope of an assignment, since that is not the focus of our work. If you encounter an unexpected vulnerability or private information, stop immediately, preserve only minimal evidence, and notify the instructor. Do not continue testing or publicly disclose the issue without authorization.

- _Use social engineering only when explicitly approved_. Do not deceive, impersonate, contact, or collect information from real people or organizations as part of an assignment unless the instructor has defined the activity and its boundaries.

- _Practice academic honesty._ Follow the course collaboration and AI policies and complete individual work yourself.

When in doubt, stop and ask the instructor or a TA before proceeding.

## Collaboration

There are _some_ group assignments for this class. In those cases, you are
encouraged to work together. Please turn in a single assignment for your group. Put all of your names on the assignment, and then we will be sure to get you all the same grade.

_Any assignment not marked as group work is designed for individual work._ In
this case, you are welcome to discuss the assignment with other class members
and how to solve it, but you may not view, share, or copy another student's
code.

## Late Policy

Late assignments earn zero credit. To provide you with some flexibility, each assignment has 2 free late days. This should give everyone flexibility to be a little late without harming their grade, e.g. for job interviews, if you are sick, etc.

You should talk to me if:

- You will have an extended absence due to illness.

- You are falling behind in the class and are in danger of failing.

- You have official university accommodations that I can help you with.

- You have any physical or mental health issues, or major life events that will impact your ability to finish.

My goal is for everyone to pass the class. Provided you are able to put in 10-12 hours per week (including lecture time), you should be able to pass.

## AI Policy

Your goal in using AI should be to help you learn, not to do your work for you. Remember, an education is all about developing your talents and your character, not about completing assignments efficiently.

The CS department is in the process of developing [common language for AI](https://claude.ai/public/artifacts/b1e35a55-f03f-4120-ba4f-b83a1ee3fc83) use in our classes. For this class, consider yourself to be at **level 2** as the default for all assignments.

- Do not use an AI tool to write or debug your code.
- Do not use an AI tool to find vulnerabilities in code (that's part of
  what you're doing in this class).

- You may use an AI tool to help understand concepts in the class.
- You may use an AI tool to help you understand code you are given that is
  unfamiliar to you ("what does this function do?").
- You may use an AI tool to give you debugging guidance, provided the tool does not fix or rewrite your code in any way.

For your final project, we will use **level 4**. You will use AI tools to do anything and everything except, of course, hack systems that you do not own.

### What constitutes cheating?

Cheating includes:

- Copying part or all of another student's project code with the exception of
  your partner(s) on group projects. This includes students from previous
  semesters.

- Breaking the AI policy.

- Copying code from online sources without crediting them. You are welcome to
  look up solutions to specific problems (e.g. how to use a library) online, but
  should not be searching for exact solutions online.

- Using or paraphrasing someone else's words without crediting them.

If you are unsure about whether or not something constitutes cheating, please
confirm with an instructor or TA.

### What happens if you cheat?

If you have chosen to be dishonest, please come talk to your instructor! I will
lovingly help you as you seek to reconcile with the truth. It will likely feel
uncomfortable and will take courage and humility, but the peace and satisfaction
of knowing you are again in the right is completely worth it. Learn for yourself
the joy of repentance.

The usual outcome for cheating on an assignment is a zero on that assignment. If
you have cheated on multiple assignments, you will receive an E for the course.

In most cases, the names of those who have cheated will be given to the Honor
Code Office.
