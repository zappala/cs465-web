# Final Project

For the final project, spend 10 to 15 hours on one of these two options:

- **Option A: Explore AI.** Use an AI coding environment to do the work for this
  class and, time permitting, to push beyond the class, and assess how capable
  AI is at these tasks.
- **Option B: Personal project.** Build a project of your own that has a
  significant security or privacy component.

This is an individual project. Start early; don't postpone it to the last week
of class.

## AI Use

For this project only, you are at level 4 of the BYU CS
[AI use guidelines](https://claude.ai/public/artifacts/b1e35a55-f03f-4120-ba4f-b83a1ee3fc83):
you may use AI tools for anything and everything, with these limits:

- **Only attack systems on your own computer.** You may use AI to attack
  things you run yourself, such as the Juice Shop in Docker or the programs
  from our exploit projects. **Do not use AI to attack any other system**,
  including pwn.college's servers, BYU systems, or anything on the internet.
  You do not have authorization for this.
- **Only redo assignments you have already turned in.** Level 4 applies only
  to work for this final project. Do not use AI on an assignment you haven't
  turned in yet, and don't submit any of this project's work for another
  assignment.
- **Keep your computer safe.** AI coding tools can run commands on your
  computer, and here they may be writing exploits. Work in a virtual machine, a
  container, or a folder used only for this project; don't give the tool your
  pwn.college login, SSH keys, or other passwords; and read each command before
  you approve it.

I recommend Claude Pro for a month, but you don't have to pay for anything:
other AI coding tools, and free tiers, are fine.

## Option A: Explore AI

Use an AI coding environment as your partner. Some examples of activities that
are encouraged:

- Redo assignments from the class that you have already turned in, and compare
  the AI's work with yours.
- Do pwn.college challenges, provided you are not pointing the AI at
  pwn.college's servers, but instead having it write code on your computer
  that you then run on pwn.college yourself.
- Create new assignments for the class, and then complete those assignments.
- Push into an area we have not covered, such as
  [Reverse Engineering](https://pwn.college/intro-to-cybersecurity/reverse-engineering/)
  or
  [Return Oriented Programming](https://pwn.college/program-security/return-oriented-programming/).

## Option B: Personal Project

Build something of your own where security or privacy is a central part of the
work, not an add-on. Some examples:

- an application that encrypts or protects user data, with a threat model
  explaining what it defends against
- a tool for finding or analyzing vulnerabilities, used only on systems you own
- hardening a server or home network you own, and testing what you did
- a privacy analysis of an app or device you own, such as what data it sends
  and to whom

You may use AI tools at level 4 (see above), but you don't have to.

## Deliverables

Submit on Canvas:

1. **A report** (PDF, at least 2 pages; be as long as you want).
   - Option A: (a) what you did with AI as a partner, (b) what you learned, and
     (c) your overall assessment of AI's capabilities for these tasks,
     including where it was wrong, wrote insecure code, or needed you to check
     its work, and what that means for security work.
   - Option B: (a) what you built, (b) its security or privacy design,
     including the threats it addresses and its limitations, (c) what you
     learned, and (d) how you used AI, if you did.
2. **A log of your time**: dates, hours, and what you worked on, totaling 10 to
   15 hours.
3. **Evidence**: examples of your work, such as excerpts from AI transcripts,
   screenshots, or code (a link to a repository is fine).
